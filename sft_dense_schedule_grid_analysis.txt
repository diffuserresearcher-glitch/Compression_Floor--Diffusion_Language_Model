================================================================
INTERPRETATION
================================================================

Q1. Is collapse a per-sample phase transition or a smooth decline?
    Per-sample phase transition. The pooled repeat-4 histogram is bimodal rather than
    hump-shaped: samples either hold together (mode near repeat-4 ~0.0-0.2) or fall into a
    locked repetition loop (second mode near ~0.8-1.0), with little mass in between. The
    CELL MEANS rise smoothly with tokens/step only because the collapsed FRACTION grows;
    individual outputs mostly do not sit at intermediate degradation. (Same signature as
    the screening rollouts: coherent openings, abrupt loop-locking.)

Q2. Is the floor schedule-driven or architecture-driven?
    The evidence points to an INTERACTION, not a pure schedule artifact. Two facts:
    (a) At slow decoding (t=1,2) the SFT model functions (boxed .20) - so a floor schedule
        exists, i.e. partly schedule-driven.
    (b) But relative to the base model AT THE SAME block size (32), SFT is strictly worse
        wherever comparison is possible: boxed .200 vs .675 at t=1, correct .175 vs .375,
        redundancy elevated at every schedule (+.21 to +.36), progress more negative at
        t<=4 - and the usable schedule range SHRANK from t<=4 (base keeps correct .150 at
        t=4) to t<=2. SFT therefore moved both the cliff position AND the ceiling level,
        which a pure decode-schedule effect would not do. Strictly separating 'training-
        data style' from architecture would need the missing block-16 SFT cells or an
        untrained-base control on this machine; not available here.

Q3. Where is the SFT-only cliff?
    Between tokens/step 2 and 4 (block 32): boxed .200 -> .000 and correct .150 -> .000,
    with coverage dropping .351 -> .237 across the same edge. Everything at t>=4 is flat
    zero on boxed/correct. (Base model's corresponding cliff at block 32 sat between t=8
    and t=16 - SFT moved it ~one octave earlier.)

Q4. How does SFT change each metric relative to the base model?
    Compared at block 32 on common schedules t=1..16 (SFT-minus-Base):
    repeat-4    : WORSE at t<=8 (+.23/+ .41/+ .39/+ .23); crossover at t=16 (-.16) where
                  both models are already past their cliffs.
    boxed rate  : much LOWER everywhere measurable (-.48 at t=1; -.33 at t=2; -.30 at t=4).
    correct rate: lower at t<=4 (-.20/-.10/-.15); equal (zero) at t>=8.
    progress    : more negative early (-.05/-.23/-.22 at t=1..4); comparable afterwards.
    redundancy  : uniformly HIGHER (+.21 to +.36) at every common schedule.
    coverage    : starts ABOVE base at t=1 (.506 vs .446 - SFT imitates reference-solution
                  phrasing more closely) but falls below it from t=2 onward.
    Net reading: SFT bought reference-style surface similarity at slow decode but reduced
    end-task accuracy and made generation markedly more repetition-prone, pulling the
    usable parallel-decoding window in from t<=4 to t<=2.
